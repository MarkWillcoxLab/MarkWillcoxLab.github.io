From **18 to 24 November**, the world marks **World AMR Awareness Week**. This year’s theme is *One Health, one action: Prevent AMR now.* The Australian Medical Association says what the clinic already knows: antimicrobial resistance is not a future threat. It is here. It is growing.

Finding one more antibiotic will not be enough. At the Mark Willcox Research Group we are working along four lines at once: **discover** new compounds, **understand** how resistance takes hold, **turn viruses against the bacteria that survive our drugs**, and **prevent** infection before it starts. Chemistry, microbiology, materials and clinical partners sit in the same conversation.

<div class="waaw-section">
<p class="waaw-kicker">Discover</p>
<h2>Designing new antibiotics, and making them safer</h2>
<p>This team designs new antibiotics with chemistry and AI, and looks at the molecules our own bodies already use to control infection. The work does not stop at a promising structure. They also ask how to make those molecules work harder, and how to take the risk out of them.</p>

<div class="waaw-section">
<p class="waaw-kicker">Understand</p>
<h2>Resistance, and the virulence that can travel with it</h2>
<p>A resistant bacterium is not only harder to treat. It may also be better at causing harm. This team is tracing how bacteria become resistant, and whether that resistance arrives hand in hand with greater virulence.</p>
<img src="{{ '/assets/images/amrweek/World_AMR_Awareness_Week_Resistance_Team_AMA_Quote.png' | relative_url }}" alt="World AMR Awareness Week poster for the resistance team: Abiye Molla, Jiawei Shen, Meseret Damtie, Shakeel Shahzad, Tanzina Akter, Abebe Adane and Yalewayker Asrat.">
<p class="small text-muted waaw-caption">Resistance and virulence — Abiye Molla, Jiawei Shen, Meseret Damtie, Shakeel Shahzad, Tanzina Akter, Abebe Adane and Yalewayker Asrat.</p>
</div>

<div class="waaw-section">
<p class="waaw-kicker">Outflank</p>
<h2>Using viruses to kill the bacteria drugs cannot</h2>
<p>When an antibiotic meets a wall, a bacteriophage can take another route. This team engineers viruses that infect and kill bacteria, including strains that have already learned to survive our medicines. They call the work what it is: bacteria, destroyed on purpose.</p>
<img src="{{ '/assets/images/amrweek/World_AMR_Awareness_Week_Bacteriophage_Team_AMA_Quote.png' | relative_url }}" alt="World AMR Awareness Week poster for the bacteriophage team: Kuma Diriba Urgeya and Suleman Irfan.">
<p class="small text-muted waaw-caption">Bacteriophage — Kuma Diriba Urgeya and Suleman Irfan.</p>
</div>

<div class="waaw-section">
<p class="waaw-kicker">Prevent</p>
<h2>From the bench to a disinfectant that works</h2>
<p>Knowledge only counts if it leaves the lab. This team tests disinfection products and develops new ones with Australian partners <strong>Lumicare</strong>, <strong>San Air</strong>, <strong>Vapour Science</strong>, <strong>MUVi</strong> and <strong>Squire Kent</strong>, and with our spin-out <strong>Surfassure</strong>. The aim is practical: fewer microbes on the surfaces and in the products people actually use.</p>

The same people sometimes stand in more than one of these pictures. That is the point. Resistance does not respect a single method, and neither does the lab.

Research today. Preserve tomorrow.
