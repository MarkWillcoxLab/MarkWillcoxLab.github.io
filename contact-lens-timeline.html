A timeline of the coating

Key milestones in antimicrobial selection, coating development, preclinical studies and human evaluation, 1998–2026.

1998–2003Selecting the agent 2008–2012Engineering melimine 2013–2014First human wear 2016–2018Mel4 on the lens 2021–2023Extended wear 2026Surfassure

Contact lens wear gives bacteria a surface. This programme asked whether a lens could carry its own antimicrobial defence, then followed that question from a protein coating and a peptide screen through animal models, human wear and a company formed to take the compounds further.

The record below keeps each step as it stands: what was tested, what was patented, and where a result was promising but not statistically significant. Two organisms recur throughout, Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus.

1998 — the first patent Lactoferrin claimed as an antimicrobial coating on biomedical devices.
The peptides Melimine, built from melittin- and protamine-derived sequences, then the shorter derivative Mel4.
2021 — 176 wearers A three-month extended-wear trial of Mel4-coated lenses. The infiltrative-event rate was lower; the difference was not statistically significant.

Selecting an antimicrobial surface

The early work tested lactoferrin on devices and lenses, screened cationic peptides against the two main ocular pathogens, and patented melimine as a coating.

  1. 1998
    Patent

    Lactoferrin as an antimicrobial coating on biomedical devices

    Biomedical devices with antimicrobial coatings

    AU5018599A

  2. 2001
    Study

    Screening cationic antimicrobial peptides

    Evaluated antimicrobial peptides and combinations against ocular Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus; identified promising leads and toxicity constraints.

    Aliwarga et al. · Clin Exp Ophthalmol · 10.1046/j.1442-9071.2001.00406.x

  3. 2001
    Patent

    Biomedical devices with antimicrobial cationic peptide and protein coatings

    AU2001245269A1

  4. 2002
    Patent

    Melimine as an antimicrobial coating for biomedical devices

    Biomedical devices with antimicrobial coatings

    US20040126409A1

  5. 2003
    Study

    Lactoferrin as an early lens-surface strategy

    Compared bacterial attachment and survival on worn and protein-coated lenses. Lactoferrin reduced the viability of attached P. aeruginosa in the tested strain.

    Williams et al. · Curr Eye Res · 10.1076/ceyr.27.4.227.16602

Engineering melimine

A hybrid cationic peptide was designed to keep broad activity while reducing toxicity, then coated lenses were tested in models of contact lens-induced acute red eye (CLARE) and contact lens-induced peripheral ulcer (CLPU). Tear-protein effects, tested in parallel, were not the same for every species.

  1. 2008
    Study

    Design of the hybrid peptide melimine

    Engineered a cationic peptide using melittin- and protamine-derived sequences, aiming to retain broad antimicrobial activity while reducing toxicity.

    Willcox et al. · J Appl Microbiol · 10.1111/j.1365-2672.2008.03942.x

  2. 2010
    Study

    Animal evidence: inflammatory complications

    Tested melimine-coated lenses in guinea pig CLARE and rabbit CLPU models, demonstrating reductions in several bacterial-associated ocular inflammatory outcomes.

    Cole et al. · Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci · 10.1167/iovs.09-4068

  3. 2011
    Study

    Protein deposition is species-dependent

    On hydrogel lens materials, lactoferrin lowered viable attached P. aeruginosa but increased S. aureus binding; protein adsorption was not uniformly protective.

    Subbaraman et al. · Optom Vis Sci · 10.1097/OPX.0b013e31821ffccb

  4. 2012
    Patent

    Attachment of melimine to biomedical devices

    Antimicrobial peptides and uses thereof

    US20140315789A1

Covalent coatings, then a day of human wear

Melimine was immobilised on etafilcon A so the coating survived heat, and it remained active against bacteria, fungi, Acanthamoeba and drug-resistant clinical isolates. The first people to wear the lenses did so for one day.

  1. 2013
    Study

    Covalent immobilisation of melimine

    Developed heat-stable melimine-coated etafilcon A lenses with activity against bacteria, fungi, Acanthamoeba and drug-resistant clinical isolates.

    Dutta et al. · Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci · 10.1167/iovs.12-10989

  2. 2014
    Study

    Rabbit safety and first human wear study

    Evaluated melimine-coated lenses in rabbits and a one-day human trial. Activity persisted after wear, although greater corneal staining was observed.

    Dutta et al. · Optom Vis Sci · 10.1097/OPX.0000000000000232

Mel4, and lenses that survive a week

Side-by-side tests on pHEMA set melimine and the shorter peptide Mel4 ahead of LL-37 and lactoferricin against both principal pathogens. Melimine-coated lenses reduced microbial keratitis in a rabbit model. Mel4 was then assessed in rabbits and a one-week human trial, and covalently coated onto silicone hydrogel materials. Peptide mimics were patented alongside that work.

  1. 2016
    Study

    Comparing immobilised antimicrobial peptides

    Compared melimine, Mel4, LL-37 and lactoferricin on pHEMA; melimine and Mel4 inhibited adhesion of both principal bacterial pathogens tested.

    Dutta et al. · Biofouling · 10.1080/08927014.2015.1129533

  2. 2016
    Study

    Protection against microbial keratitis in rabbits

    In a contact lens-associated P. aeruginosa model, melimine-coated lenses reduced microbial keratitis incidence and improved clinical signs.

    Dutta et al. · Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci · 10.1167/iovs.16-19882

  3. 2017
    Patent

    Peptide mimics as antimicrobial compounds

    Antimicrobial compounds

    US20190256466A1

  4. 2017
    Study

    Mel4: antimicrobial activity and biocompatibility

    Evaluated shorter melimine-derived Mel4 against additional Gram-negative genera and assessed Mel4-coated lenses in rabbits and a one-week human trial.

    Dutta et al. · Cont Lens Anterior Eye · 10.1016/j.clae.2017.01.002

  5. 2018
    Study

    Applying Mel4 to silicone hydrogel lenses

    Covalently coated multiple silicone hydrogel materials, assessing antimicrobial activity, physical lens parameters and short-term rabbit biocompatibility.

    Dutta et al. · Optom Vis Sci · 10.1097/OPX.0000000000001282

Three months of extended wear

A three-month trial in 176 participants recorded fewer corneal infiltrative events with Mel4; the difference was not statistically significant. Comfort matched the control lenses, and lenses recovered after wear had lost antibacterial activity. A further peptide-mimic patent was filed in the same period.

  1. 2021
    Patent

    New peptide mimics as antimicrobial compounds

    Novel broad spectrum antiviral and antimicrobial peptide mimics

    AU2021902457A0

  2. 2021
    Study

    Randomised clinical trial of Mel4 lenses

    A 176-participant, three-month extended-wear trial found a 69% lower corneal infiltrative event incidence rate with Mel4; the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.29).

    Kalaiselvan et al. · Transl Vis Sci Technol · 10.1167/tvst.10.7.32

  3. 2022
    Study

    Extended-wear comfort and biocompatibility

    The clinical follow-up found similar ocular surface measures and comfort with Mel4-coated and control lenses during the three-month extended-wear study.

    Kalaiselvan et al. · Antibiotics · 10.3390/antibiotics11010058

  4. 2023
    Study

    Durability limitation during extended wear

    Ex vivo tests showed loss of antibacterial activity after approximately six nights of wear; proteolytic degradation was implicated as a likely cause.

    Kalaiselvan et al. · Nanomaterials · 10.3390/nano13020349

From the lens to a company

The compounds left the laboratory timeline as a commercial programme, with contact lens coatings named as one application.

  1. 2026
    Commercial

    Launch of Surfassure Pty Ltd and Surfassure Hong Kong

    Launched to commercialise the antimicrobial compounds, with one application as coatings for contact lenses.
